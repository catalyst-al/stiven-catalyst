There is an undeniable truth that must be said without the slightest hesitation: the system is often unjust. Many businesses are run without vision, wages stay below human dignity, nepotism kills meritocracy and corruption erodes the hope of honest people every single day. To recognise these wounds, to name them out loud and to refuse to dress them up is not pessimism; it is the first step towards intellectual honesty.

But precisely here, at this station where the bitter truth becomes clear, an invisible psychological abyss opens up. It is the sweet and insidious trap of victimhood.

Picture an ordinary conversation in a café after an exhausting day at work. People sit around the table, meticulously count every injustice of the owner, every incompetence of the state, every flaw of this country, and in the end, with a heavy sigh, they reach the familiar conclusion: “Nothing ever gets done here. What can we do? We are just small people.”

Let me say this with all the weight of human care. **There is a dangerous comfort in the role of the victim. Because if everything is someone else's fault, then you are excused from any obligation to act.**

Let us analyse this calmly.

When you convince yourself that your fate depends entirely on the mercy of an arrogant owner or on the repair of a political system, you have won moral innocence, but you have surrendered power over your own life. If the fault is theirs alone, then you have no reason to tire yourself out learning a new skill in the evening. You have no reason to read, no reason to understand how the world works beyond the walls of that warehouse, and no reason to build a plan to climb out of the mud. Complaint turns into a kind of drug that soothes today's pain but guarantees tomorrow's paralysis.

And here lies the greatest tragedy: by keeping your focus only on the other person's fault, you turn the person who oppresses you into the centre of your universe.

Your mood depends on him. Your future depends on him. Your day is decided by his whims. In trying to hate him, you end up bound to him by chains stronger than the employment contract itself. You become a bitter spectator of your own decline, while the years slip away and your energy burns out in barren debates around café tables, with no result at all.

True rebellion, the kind that changes a person's fate, does not begin with lament. It begins with taking back personal responsibility.

You may not be able to change your owner's mentality by tomorrow morning. You cannot wipe out the corruption of an entire society with a stroke of the pen. But there is a sovereign territory, however narrow it may seem at first, that no one can enter without your permission: the territory of your own preparation.

What do you do with the two hours left to you after work? What do you read? What do you learn? How do you discipline your mind? Are you coming to understand how a modern logistics system works, how an operating procedure is written, how data is managed, or how a new body of knowledge is mastered with professional precision?

When you choose to invest in your own strength instead of lamenting the weakness of others, the equation changes at its root. You are no longer the defenceless employee waiting for a crumb of kindness from above. You become a professional who is gradually building his own unshakeable value, a value that sooner or later will find its way out through that door.

True freedom does not come when the system becomes perfect; freedom begins on the day you decide to stop being a hostage to its imperfection.

Those who lead with arrogance feed on your surrender. They want you to believe that you are powerless, that you have no other choice and that the world beyond their fence does not exist. And every time you accept the role of the hopeless victim, you are giving them exactly what they want.

So let us keep our conscience clean. Let us keep demanding justice, condemning exploitation and refusing every form of humiliation. But never, not even once, let us allow someone else's injustice to become our excuse for abandoning ourselves.

Because when all is said and done, your effort is yours, your mind is your own, and the only life you have been given on this earth is far too precious to be handed over to someone else's fault.
