There is a particular kind of human smallness, a deep insecurity that cannot be hidden behind expensive cars or offices with tinted glass. And this smallness does not show when work goes badly, but precisely when the ordinary employee takes a little breath of freedom.

Picture for a moment a scene that happens all the time in our daily life. It is midsummer. An employee, after eleven months of sweat on shifts without set hours, finally takes five days of leave. They go with their family somewhere by the sea, take a photo smiling and post it on social media. A simple, human, modest moment.

But far from there, on a leather sofa or behind a desk, someone is zooming in on that photo with a finger on the phone screen. The owner is doing the arithmetic.

The brows come together, the eyes narrow, and instead of an ordinary, decent thought, a poisonous question is born: *How is this possible? On the wage I pay, how can this one afford five days in a hotel? Are they stealing from me? Are they laughing at me behind my back?*

Let me be completely direct about this absurdity. **When you employ a human being, you pay for the service and the time they give within working hours. You do not buy their life, you do not buy their breath, and above all you have no right to decide how much joy they are allowed outside the walls of your business.**

This kind of digital spying on staff holidays is not a sign of managerial vigilance. It is the mindset of a slave owner dressed in modern technology.

That owner cannot understand how the employee can go on holiday, because in their head the wage they pay was never meant as a means to live with dignity. It was calculated with spiteful precision just to secure survival, just to keep the body on its feet so it comes back to work on Monday. And when they see that the employee dares to buy a coffee by the sea or have dinner with their children, their scheme of submission is shaken.

They do not know, and do not want to know, that this person may have saved coin by coin for a whole year. They do not know that a brother abroad may have helped, or that the person may have gone into debt just to give their children a few days of good memories. For the small leader, the employee should have no parallel life, no dreams and no other source of hope besides the company's alms.

**At its core, this is not financial suspicion; it is pure jealousy of another person's freedom.**

It is the fear that the employee, looking at the horizon for a few days without the boss's voice over their head, will realise that the world is much bigger than that warehouse or that shop. It is the fear that a person, once they have tasted calm, will no longer agree to come back submissive. Because weak power stays standing only on desperate people, who have no time either to think or to rest.

A true leader is glad to see their people living well. A manager with integrity measures their own success by the wellbeing of the team around them too, knowing well that a person who rests in peace comes back with more energy, more respect and a clearer mind.

But those who sit up at night counting their workers' beach umbrellas on screens have lost all touch with leadership. They are simply the guards of an imaginary prison, condemned to live in constant fear that someone might be happier than they are.

And the greatest truth remains this: you may hold the keys to the safe and the codes to the accounts, but if your soul shrinks before five days of sun for an ordinary worker, then the truly poor one in that picture is not the person counting coins on the beach. **The truly poor one is you.**
