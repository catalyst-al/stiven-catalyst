# stiven-catalyst
Modern publication platform for leadership, operations, management insights, field notes and personal projects by Stiven Janaqi.

## How the site works
The site is built with [Eleventy](https://www.11ty.dev/) from the `src/` folder and deployed to GitHub Pages by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` on every push to `main`.

- `src/content/insights/`, `src/content/notes/`, `src/content/projects/`: one Markdown file per essay, field note and project.
- `src/_data/`: site settings (`site.json`), homepage highlights (`home.json`) and Impressum details (`legal.json`).
- `src/_includes/`: the shared layout (head, header, footer), the article layout and the newsletter block.
- `src/media/`: uploaded images and files.
- `src/tools/` and `src/js/`: the free tools (KPI Diagnostic, 5 Whys, Damage Control, Incomplete Control, Delay Analyzer, Sigma & Control Chart, Shift Handover, Pareto 80/20, Last-Mile CX Control Tower, CV Builder, ATS CV, Cover Letter) and the Six Sigma in the Warehouse course. Their cards come from `src/_data/tools.json`; the diagnostic's statements and advice live in `src/_data/kpiDiagnostic.json`. Damage Control and Incomplete Control share one page partial (`partials/defect-log.njk`) and script (`js/defect-log.js`); everything that differs (wording, fields, stage and cause advice, floor check, example week) lives in `src/_data/damageControl.json` and `src/_data/incompleteControl.json`. Both take an optional volume for each day (typed, pasted from Excel, or in the example; saved as `volumes` in the log, read through `cleanVolumes`, `importVolumes` and `dayVolumes` in `tool-kit.js`): with it the trend chart is a rate per day or week with the target drawn as a line (columns above it in red), days with entries but no volume are left out and listed, and the volume of the period fills itself when every day with entries has one. Delay Analyzer has its own page and script (`js/delay-analyzer.js`), with reasons, advice, floor check and example week in `src/_data/delayAnalyzer.json`. Sigma & Control Chart (`js/sigma-chart.js`, `src/_data/sigmaChart.json`) holds the sigma and perfect delivery calculators and a p-chart with three signal rules, read by day or by week (the days added up from each Monday, `byWeek`; the signal texts say weeks). Damage Control and Incomplete Control (with daily volumes) and Delay Analyzer (when every route of the period is logged) open their days in it with "Open as control chart" through `sessionStorage` (`sc-sigma-handoff`): the same metric and unit are added to the chart, anything else replaces it, and Undo puts the old days back. The course (`tools/six-sigma-dmaic.njk`, `js/dmaic.js`) takes its steps, case, quiz, eight wastes, glossary and charter fields from `src/_data/dmaic.json`. Shift Handover (`js/shift-handover.js`, `src/_data/shiftHandover.json`) has templates for warehouse, hotel front office and delivery station, and carries open issues into the next shift. The warehouse tools share small helpers in `js/tool-kit.js`: dates follow the device clock (a night shift after midnight logs the new day), and Damage Control, Incomplete Control and Delay Analyzer analyse a chosen period (today, 7 or 30 days, all, or two dates; `partials/log-range.njk`) so that a volume or route count is compared with the rows of the same days. A removed log row can be put back for a few seconds. In the Delay Analyzer a departure inside its grace counts no minutes at the dock, the "How late" bands start after the arrival grace, a route shows whether it is late while it is typed, and when only some routes of a period are logged, hours and shifts show counts instead of shares; its route arithmetic (`window.DelayMath`) and the shared helpers are tested in `tests/pulse-tools.test.cjs`. The three log tools share one engine in `tool-kit.js` (`logBook`: settings and period, the table, removing with undo, pasting, the example, clearing and the CSV), and their results come answer first (`resultLayout`: headline, key numbers, where to start and the actions, then "All details", closed on a phone). Shift Pulse (`tools/shift-pulse.njk`, `partials/shift-pulse.njk`, `js/shift-pulse.js`) is Pulse's one-look page: it reads the three logs and the open issues of the handover in progress from `localStorage` (nothing of its own is stored but the period and shift choice), shows damage, incomplete orders and on-time routes as cards with a column sparkline, flags a latest day clearly above the usual, draws a trend chart per number (`trendBuckets`, `trendShares` and `trendFigure` in `tool-kit.js`, which Damage Control, Incomplete Control and Delay Analyzer use for their own trend panels; each chart exports as PNG or SVG) and copies or prints a summary. It reads the last 7, 14 or 30 days, or the last 8 or 12 weeks (each week from its Monday, the latest one the week so far); with the volume of each day in the two defect logs, and not for a single shift, the cards and charts show rates and the target of each log as a line. It is also on Zenith's orbit, for the area manager's week. Its arithmetic is `window.PulseMath`, tested in `tests/pulse-tools.test.cjs`. Shift Handover fills its numbers (damage %, incomplete orders, on-time departures or arrivals) from those logs for its own date and shift (`sources` in `src/_data/shiftHandover.json`), without overwriting anything typed. With long logs the tools stay quick: the table shows the newest 25 rows ("Show more" adds 100, the CSV has all), the log is written to storage just after the screen updates (at once if the page is hidden or closed), and parts of a tool page that are off screen are not laid out until they come near it (`content-visibility`). The coaching curriculum (`src/_data/coaching.js`) is served the same way, as `/js/coaching-data.js` (`src/coaching-data.njk`): role pages load it with the workspace, and a tool page loads it, with the coaching panel's scripts, only when it was opened from a coaching project (`?coaching=`), so an ordinary tool visit downloads none of it. German and Albanian tool pages load their interface texts from one cached file, `/js/ui-de.js` or `/js/ui-sq.js` (`src/ui-strings.njk`), instead of carrying them in every page. The Tools page and every tool page end with "All your work in one file" (`partials/work-backup.njk`, `js/work-backup.js`): every `sc-` key in `localStorage` except the theme is saved to one JSON file and can be opened on another device, replacing only the tools in the file; it is tested in `tests/work-backup.test.cjs`. CV Builder (`tools/cv-builder.njk`, `partials/cv-builder.njk`, `js/cv-builder.js`) lays a CV out on A4 pages in the browser and prints it as a PDF; its colour themes, CV headings in English, German and Albanian, and the example CVs live in `src/_data/cvBuilder.json`. A company website typed there is the one thing a tool sends out: its logo is loaded from Google's icon service (see Datenschutz). ATS CV (`tools/ats-cv.njk`, `partials/ats-cv.njk`, `js/ats-cv.js`, `src/_data/atsCv.json`) makes a plain one-column CV, matches it to a job ad and saves it as PDF, Word or text; it can start from an old CV, which `js/cv-import.js` reads in the browser from a PDF (with pdf.js, kept in `js/vendor/pdfjs/`), a Word file or pasted text. Cover Letter (`tools/cover-letter.njk`, `partials/cover-letter.njk`, `js/cover-letter.js`, `src/_data/coverLetter.json`) writes the letter in the design of either CV and takes the details from them. Both write Word files with `js/docx-kit.js`. Pareto 80/20 (`tools/pareto.njk`, `partials/pareto.njk`, `js/pareto.js`, `src/_data/pareto.json`) ranks causes from a CSV, an Excel file (.xlsx, unzipped in the browser by `js/cv-import.js`), pasted cells or one of eleven templates for logistics, warehouse and hotel work; it lists every row it leaves out, merges spellings only when asked, and exports PNG, SVG and CSV. Damage Control, Incomplete Control and Delay Analyzer open their logs in it through `sessionStorage` (`sc-pareto-handoff`); its engine is tested in `tests/pareto.test.cjs`. Last-Mile CX Control Tower (`tools/cx-control-tower.njk`, `partials/cx-tower.njk`, `js/cx-tower.js`, `src/_data/cxTower.json`) reads four tables (deliveries, routes, incidents, actions) from CSV files or from the sheets of one Excel workbook, recognising each by its column names, and takes targets from a “Metric | Target” sheet. It computes Perfect Delivery per delivery, the other KPIs with their own denominators, partner and driver scorecards with a minimum sample, a route risk queue, a root-cause Pareto (drawn with the Pareto tool's chart and handed to it through `sessionStorage`), corrective actions and a weekly review; KPIs without an approved target are shown but never scored. Its engine is tested in `tests/cx-tower.test.cjs`. Before the print window, all three CV tools show a short guide to saving the PDF (`js/print-guide.js`). The homepage points to the three career tools (`partials/career-feature.njk`). They run entirely in the browser; logs are kept only in the visitor's `localStorage`.
- The planets are roles. The homepage hero (`partials/cosmos-hero.njk`, `js/cosmos.js`) draws the Catalyst light with one planet per role on its orbits. The bulb hangs from the top of the hero and swings like a pendant lamp when the system is dragged (a pendulum with gravity and damping, and a light tilt with the mouse), throwing a pool of light on the plane of the orbits that moves with it; its filament is a sound wave, the voice of the brand, which pulses as it glows and speaks up when the bulb is tapped. A small reader, a pictogram without a face (one warm colour, thick rounded limbs, the head apart from the body), sits on two books on the inner orbit in the pool of light and reads an open book, turning a page now and then; it looks up, stretches or walks a few steps, and left alone for a minute it dozes off over its book while the light dims a little (the pointer wakes it). When a planet is opened it runs there along the beam and stands on it, leaving the books at its seat, when a moon is tapped it jumps to the moon as the tool opens, and when the planet is closed it comes back and sits down; with reduced motion it only sits, or stands on the open planet. The planets sit, and an orbit widens as the role's view does: Pulse is the Shift Lead (one shift), Zenith the Area Manager (one area, week by week), Lumen the Process Manager (the whole system), and Atlas the career that every role carries. The roles live in `src/_data/families.json`: name, role, scope, colour and orbit; `tools` (the tools whose home is this planet), `learn` (training modules, such as the Six Sigma course on Lumen), `also` (tools borrowed from other planets), `read` (essays, by file slug), `routine` (when each tool is used: a moment, what to do, which tool) and `next` (the next orbit). Each planet has its own physics and its moons are its tools (one moon per tool or training module): Pulse cracks and beats inside a ring that is a heartbeat trace, Zenith is a contour map under a radar sweep with a polar cap, Lumen shines by itself inside a ring of dust in five parts (the DMAIC steps), Atlas is a globe with meridians, half in the light, with the path of a career drawn as a constellation on its lit side. The same four planets are drawn as SVG on the role cards, the Tools page and the role pages (`partials/family-planet.njk`, shapes from `src/_data/familySky.js`: `planets`), where Pulse beats, Zenith's radar sweeps and Lumen's ring turns in CSS. Dragging turns and tilts the system, a tap on a planet or its button opens that role's tools and a link to its planet, and a tap on a moon opens that tool (the mouse shows its name first; the moons are read from the panel's list, so they stay the same links). It pauses when off screen and stays still for visitors who prefer reduced motion. Every role has its own page, `/roles/<slug>/` in each language (`src/roles.njk`, `src/de/roles.njk`, `src/sq/roles.njk`, all through `partials/role-page.njk`), in four rings: Use (its tools and the ones it borrows), Routine, Learn (training modules and essays) and Grow (the next orbit, and Atlas for the CV). The Tools page shows the same roles (`partials/tool-families.njk`): a card per role with its own planet (`partials/family-cards.njk`, also on the homepage, where each card opens the role's page), then each role with its tools. A tool that no role lists yet appears under "More tools"; `tests/families.test.cjs` checks that every tool has exactly one home in all three languages, that borrowed tools and routine steps exist, and that every text is translated. Every tool page carries its role (`familyOf` in `eleventy.config.js`, set in `layouts/base.njk`): a drawn background above the title (`partials/family-stage.njk`, shapes from `src/_data/familySky.js`, fitted to the title by `js/family-sky.js`), a badge that opens the role's page, and "More from …" with the role's other tools at the end (`partials/family-more.njk`). Lumen has a beam of light with drifting dust, Pulse a monitor grid and a heartbeat along the foot of the title, Zenith contour lines and a radar, Atlas a constellation and a compass; a training module has five moons that light up as the DMAIC steps are completed. The role's own planet (the same SVG as on the cards) stands in the open space beside the title on every tool and role page, and on a training module at the start of the moons' arc; `js/family-sky.js` measures the title's lines, the eyebrow, the badge and the fixed parts of the drawing and moves the planet when its body (disc, rings and moons, not its glow) would touch a word: it stays where the stylesheet puts it when it can, else goes beside the title past its longest line, or above it towards the right, at full size first and then smaller, and on a phone the corner above the title comes first. The decision is written to `data-spot` on the planet, and `#sky-debug` in the address adds a `data-trace`. The drawings stay around the title, stop for reduced motion and are left out of print. Inside the tool the role colour is the accent too (labels, links, focus, selected answers, progress and the main buttons), while colours that carry meaning (right and wrong, better and worse, priority, warnings, the ticks in Pulse) keep a separate signal colour and the CV documents keep their own themes. When a tool page, a role page or the Tools page is shared, its preview is a card with the name, the planet and its drawing, in the page's language: `src/media/social/<lang>/<tool>.jpg`, `role-<slug>.jpg` and `tools.jpg`; the homepage, and every page without an image of its own, shares `home.jpg`, a photograph of the hero with the light and the planets on their orbits. All are made by `node scripts/social-cards.mjs` (it needs Playwright; run it again after changing a tool's name, summary or role; a test checks that every card exists).
- A first visit to a log tool (Damage Control, Incomplete Control, Delay Analyzer, the Sigma chart), Pareto or the CX Control Tower opens on its example, so the result is visible at once: nothing is written to the browser until the visitor acts, and a visit sent by a coaching project (`?coaching=`) skips the example so it cannot become the project's evidence (`firstVisit` in `tool-kit.js`). The log tools and the chart show a note above the result (`exampleNote` in `tool-kit.js`) with one tap to clear it; the example is remembered by its shape (`exampleStamp`: rows, first and last date, kept as `example` in the saved state), so the note goes by itself the moment the log differs from it. Pareto names its source "Example", the Control Tower keeps its own example flag.
- Tool pages carry a `seo_title` in English and German (the search phrase a shift lead would type, such as "Schadensquote berechnen"); the page's `title` stays the tool's name for sharing, cards and structured data.
- Each tool's card (`partials/tool-card.njk`, on the Tools page and the role pages) opens with a picture of the tool's result: `src/media/tools/<lang>/<tool>.jpg`, made by `node scripts/tool-previews.mjs` (it builds nothing itself: run `npm run build` first; it needs Playwright, loads each tool's example or fills a short case, and photographs the result; a test checks that every picture exists; run it again after a tool's result changes). The `toolPreview` filter finds the picture, `imageSize` its size.
- Essays and tools point at each other: an English essay lists the tools it is about in `relatedTools` (English addresses; the key is not `tools`, which would merge with `tools.json`), the German and Albanian essays follow their English original. `layouts/article.njk` shows "Tools for this essay" under the essay, `partials/tool-essays.njk` shows "Read the story behind this tool" on the tool page, both in the page's language. Under that, every essay shows where it stands in the series ("Ten years close to the work · Essay 4 of 5") with the previous and the next published essay in its language (`seriesNeighbours` in `eleventy.config.js`; essays marked `soon` are not counted). The editorial plan for the series, the LinkedIn versions of each essay and the distribution kit (posting rhythm, a German post per tool, profile texts) are in `docs/seria-dhjete-vite-prane-punes.md`, `docs/linkedin-seria.md` and `docs/shperndarja-linkedin.md`.
- Every tool page ends with one feedback question, "Did this tool help you?" (`partials/tool-feedback.njk`, `js/feedback.js`): yes or no, an optional text and an optional email. With `contact.action` in `src/_data/site.json` set to a form provider's address (Formspree, Web3Forms or the like; `contact.provider` names it in the privacy policy), the answer is posted there with the tool's English address, the language and the page, and the provider's origin is added to the page's `connect-src`; without one, the answer goes through the visitor's own mail program to `email`. A hidden field catches bots. `tests/feedback.test.cjs` covers both paths.
- Every page has a share planet at the bottom right (`partials/share-orbit.njk`, `js/share-orbit.js`): it opens WhatsApp, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, email and copy link as small planets, plus the phone's own share sheet where there is one. They are plain links, no third-party scripts.
- `src/books/` (and `de/`, `sq/`): the book *Mall për Durrësin*, read page by page like a real book (`pages/book.njk`, `js/book.js`). The page images, chapters and text come from the PDF through `scripts/book-pages.py`, which writes `src/media/books/mall-per-durresin/` and `src/_data/book.json`. The book also appears on the homepage, the Reflections page and the About page (`partials/book-feature.njk`).
- `.pages.yml`: the editing panel at [app.pagescms.org](https://app.pagescms.org). See `UDHEZUES.md` (in Albanian) for how to add content.

## Run locally
```
npm install
npm start      # http://localhost:8080/
npm run build  # writes the site to _site/
```

## Coaching workspace
The four role pages now include a working coaching workspace: 13 original modules, contextual cases, workplace assignments, saved projects, action plans, evidence, before/after measurements, GROW reflections and printable project reports. The original tools can attach a visitor-written summary to the active project. Work stays in the visitor's browser, with validated backup import/export; there are no accounts or team sync. See [docs/COACHING_PLATFORM.md](docs/COACHING_PLATFORM.md) for the visitor journey, architecture, data rules and validation.

Run `npm test`, `npm run build` and `node scripts/check-coaching-build.mjs` to check the coaching workflows and the generated role/tool pages in all three languages.

## Notes
- Links in templates are written from the site root (`/styles.css`); The site lives at https://stivencatalyst.com/ (`url` in `src/_data/site.json`); Eleventy prefixes links with the path in that address, so moving the site means changing `url` there and everything follows (links, social previews, sitemap, feed).
- After each build `lib/css-split.js` minifies `styles.css` and writes `styles-light.css` for every page that is not a tool or role page: the same rules, minus those that need a class that appears nowhere in those pages or the scripts they load (about 13 KB instead of 34 KB gzipped). Tool and role pages keep the whole stylesheet in its original order. Its rules are tested in `tests/css-split.test.cjs`.
- Insights and Field Notes have client-side search and topic filters (`src/js/filter.js`); the filter is kept in the URL (`?topic=Operations&q=handoff`) so it can be shared. Essays show a reading time that is counted from the text unless one is typed in.
- Every page carries structured data for search engines (`partials/structured-data.njk`, JSON-LD): the author as a Person and the site as its publisher on every page, a WebSite on the homepage, a ProfilePage on About, an Article (headline, description, dates, language, section, reading time, image, author) on every essay and reflection, a SoftwareApplication (free web application, its kind from `tools.json` as sub-category, the time it takes) on every tool page, a Course with its workload on a training module, and an ItemList of the tools on the Tools page. The author's profile links (LinkedIn and the like) go in `profiles` in `src/_data/site.json` and appear as `sameAs`.
- Every page carries a Content Security Policy as a `<meta>` tag (`layouts/base.njk`; GitHub Pages cannot send headers): scripts, styles, fonts and images come from this site only, the two inline scripts (the theme bootstrap and the coaching loader, `partials/coaching-loader.njk`) are allowed by their hash (`cspHash` filter), images may also be `data:`/`blob:` (exports, uploaded photos) or the company logo from Google's icon service, pdf.js runs its worker from here, and forms may post to an `https:` provider. A new inline script needs its hash in the policy, or a file of its own.
- `imageSize` (a filter) reads the pixel size of a JPEG, PNG or WebP in `src/`, so the essay image and project images carry `width` and `height`.
- The newsletter section stays hidden until `newsletter.action` is set in `src/_data/site.json`.
- The legal notice and privacy pages (`src/impressum.njk`, `src/datenschutz.njk`) come in English, German and Albanian (`/impressum.html`, `/de/impressum.html`, `/sq/impressum.html` and the same for `datenschutz`) and are only generated once the name and city in `src/_data/legal.json` are filled in. They are `noindex`, with a language switch but no hreflang links.
- `src/fonts/` holds Archivo Black (SIL OFL), used only on devices without Arial Black (Android, Linux).
