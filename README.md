# stiven-catalyst
Modern publication platform for leadership, operations, management insights, field notes and personal projects by Stiven Janaqi.

## How the site works
The site is built with [Eleventy](https://www.11ty.dev/) from the `src/` folder and deployed to GitHub Pages by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` on every push to `main`.

- `src/content/insights/`, `src/content/notes/`, `src/content/projects/`: one Markdown file per essay, field note and project.
- `src/_data/`: site settings (`site.json`), homepage highlights (`home.json`) and Impressum details (`legal.json`).
- `src/_includes/`: the shared layout (head, header, footer), the article layout and the newsletter block.
- `src/media/`: uploaded images and files.
- `src/tools/` and `src/js/`: the free tools (KPI Diagnostic, 5 Whys, Damage Control, Incomplete Control, Night Audit Log, Delay Analyzer, Sigma & Control Chart, Shift Handover, Shift Pulse, Pareto 80/20, Last-Mile CX Control Tower, CV Builder, ATS CV, Cover Letter) and the Six Sigma in the Warehouse course. Their cards come from `src/_data/tools.json`; the diagnostic's statements and advice live in `src/_data/kpiDiagnostic.json`. Damage Control, Incomplete Control and the Night Audit Log (the hotel front office: no-shows, declined cards, posting errors and open balances by stage of the stay and cause, with `paretoTemplate` naming the Pareto template it hands over to) share one page partial (`partials/defect-log.njk`) and script (`js/defect-log.js`); everything that differs (wording, fields, stage and cause advice, floor check, example week) lives in `src/_data/damageControl.json`, `src/_data/incompleteControl.json` and `src/_data/nightAudit.json`. Both take an optional volume for each day (typed, pasted from Excel, or in the example; saved as `volumes` in the log, read through `cleanVolumes`, `importVolumes` and `dayVolumes` in `tool-kit.js`): with it the trend chart is a rate per day or week with the target drawn as a line (columns above it in red), days with entries but no volume are left out and listed, and the volume of the period fills itself when every day with entries has one. Delay Analyzer has its own page and script (`js/delay-analyzer.js`), with reasons, advice, floor check and example week in `src/_data/delayAnalyzer.json`. Sigma & Control Chart (`js/sigma-chart.js`, `src/_data/sigmaChart.json`) holds the sigma and perfect delivery calculators and a p-chart with three signal rules, read by day or by week (the days added up from each Monday, `byWeek`; the signal texts say weeks). Damage Control and Incomplete Control (with daily volumes) and Delay Analyzer (when every route of the period is logged) open their days in it with "Open as control chart" through `sessionStorage` (`sc-sigma-handoff`): the same metric and unit are added to the chart, anything else replaces it (so does an example still on the chart: real days never mix with it), and Undo puts the old days back. The course (`tools/six-sigma-dmaic.njk`, `js/dmaic.js`) takes its steps, case, quiz, eight wastes, glossary and charter fields from `src/_data/dmaic.json`. Shift Handover (`js/shift-handover.js`, `src/_data/shiftHandover.json`) has templates for warehouse, hotel front office and delivery station, and carries open issues into the next shift. "Send to the next shift" moves a handover to another device without a server (`window.ShiftHandoverLink`, `tests/handover-link.test.cjs`, in the browser `tests/browser/handover-transfer.spec.mjs`): the dialog first shows what goes (the numbers, the open and solved issues, the heads-up lines, the checks; the names of people stay out unless ticked, and what looks like an email address, a phone number, a card number or an IBAN is pointed out, `sensitiveFindings`), then seals the handover with a transfer code (six words of the team-code list, about 62 bits, or one typed, at least eight characters, with a warning when it is weak: short, digits only, repeated, a run like 12345678, a word everyone tries first, `weakPasscode`; PBKDF2-SHA-256 with 310 000 rounds and a random salt → AES-GCM 256 with the format bound as additional data, `packHandover`, bytes `[2, salt, iv, ciphertext]`). The sealed bytes travel after `#h2=` in the address (base64url; a QR code drawn by `TeamSyncMath.qrEncode` when they fit, up to 2331 bytes), through the share sheet or a copied link, or as a small file (`stiven-catalyst-handover-file`, `fileText`) when they do not; the code is told separately, so the link alone, in a chat history or a screenshot, is unreadable. The handover counts as shared only on the sender's own act that succeeded (a link the clipboard confirmed it took, `copyText` checks the older command's answer too, a share sheet that returned, a saved file, "I showed the QR code"), and the record (`shared: [{ id, at, mode, ack }]`) carries the confirmation code expected, never the transfer code. The receiving page asks for the code, shows what the handover holds and its issues beside the ones open there (`mergeIssues`: new, already open here, kept from here; the same text is not the same issue: an incoming issue is taken for one open here only when the texts match, the owner and the due date agree or are missing on one side, and exactly one such issue is here; then it stays one issue and the safer state wins: open even when the sender marked it solved, a missing owner or date filled from here, the higher priority and carry count kept, each difference shown under the issue; when the owner or the date differ, or more than one issue here could be the one, every record is kept and the incoming one is marked ambiguous), says when the same transfer was received before (`state.received`), and replaces nothing unasked: what was there moves to the history, the open issues the sender did not have stay. Once the receiver confirms takeover, a six-character confirmation code (`ackCode`: SHA-256 of the transfer id and the code, so only a device that opened the handover can make it) is shown to give back by voice, message or a link (`#a=`); typed on the sender's device, it turns "Shared, not yet confirmed by the other device" into "Acknowledged: confirmation code typed in by hand". It is a manual confirmation that the handover was opened with its transfer code, not a proof of who opened it or on which device, and the page says so. The statuses are draft, ready, shared, received, taken over (this device) and acknowledged. A wrong code or a changed byte opens nothing (AES-GCM cannot tell the two apart, and the message says so); a fragment past 64 KB, a file past 1 MB or a payload past 256 KB is refused; the old plain links (`#h=`, `#h0=`) are still read, with a note that they were made without encryption, and are no longer made. The warehouse tools share small helpers in `js/tool-kit.js`: dates follow the device clock (a night shift after midnight logs the new day), and Damage Control, Incomplete Control and Delay Analyzer analyse a chosen period (today, 7 or 30 days, all, or two dates; `partials/log-range.njk`) so that a volume or route count is compared with the rows of the same days. A total typed for the period (units handled, routes; `data-period-total`) remembers the days it was typed for (`totalsFor`, read through `periodTotal`): with other days chosen it is not used, the result says for which days it was typed, and the daily volumes or a count of late routes stand in. A removed log row can be put back for a few seconds. In the Delay Analyzer a departure inside its grace counts no minutes at the dock, the "How late" bands start after the arrival grace, a route shows whether it is late while it is typed, and when only some routes of a period are logged, hours and shifts show counts instead of shares; its route arithmetic (`window.DelayMath`) and the shared helpers are tested in `tests/pulse-tools.test.cjs`. The three log tools share one engine in `tool-kit.js` (`logBook`: settings and period, the table, removing with undo, pasting, the example, clearing and the CSV), and their results come answer first (`resultLayout`: headline, key numbers, where to start and the actions, then "All details", closed on a phone). Shift Pulse (`tools/shift-pulse.njk`, `partials/shift-pulse.njk`, `js/shift-pulse.js`) is Pulse's one-look page: it reads the three logs and the open issues of the handover in progress from `localStorage` (nothing of its own is stored but the period and shift choice), shows damage, incomplete orders and on-time routes as cards with a column sparkline, flags a latest day clearly above the usual (beyond the three-sigma limit of the control chart: a c-chart for a count, a p-chart for a rate at that day's own volume, so a usual day of a stable process is not flagged), draws a trend chart per number (`trendBuckets`, `trendShares` and `trendFigure` in `tool-kit.js`, which Damage Control, Incomplete Control and Delay Analyzer use for their own trend panels; each chart exports as PNG or SVG) and copies or prints a summary. It reads the last 7, 14 or 30 days, or the last 8 or 12 weeks (each week from its Monday, the latest one the week so far); with the volume of each day in the two defect logs, and not for a single shift, the cards and charts show rates and the target of each log as a line. It is also on Zenith's orbit, for the area manager's week. Its arithmetic is `window.PulseMath`, tested in `tests/pulse-tools.test.cjs`. Shift Handover fills its numbers (damage %, incomplete orders, on-time departures or arrivals) from those logs for its own date and shift (`sources` in `src/_data/shiftHandover.json`), without overwriting anything typed. With long logs the tools stay quick: the table shows the newest 25 rows ("Show more" adds 100, the CSV has all), the log is written to storage just after the screen updates (at once if the page is hidden or closed), and parts of a tool page that are off screen are not laid out until they come near it (`content-visibility`). The coaching curriculum (`src/_data/coaching.js`) is served the same way, as `/js/coaching-data.js` (`src/coaching-data.njk`): role pages load it with the workspace, and a tool page loads it, with the coaching panel's scripts, only when it was opened from a coaching project (`?coaching=`), so an ordinary tool visit downloads none of it. German and Albanian tool pages load their interface texts from one cached file, `/js/ui-de.js` or `/js/ui-sq.js` (`src/ui-strings.njk`), instead of carrying them in every page. The Tools page and every tool page end with "All your work in one file" (`partials/work-backup.njk`, `js/work-backup.js`): every `sc-` key in `localStorage` except the theme is saved to one JSON file and can be opened on another device, replacing only the tools in the file; it is tested in `tests/work-backup.test.cjs`. CV Builder (`tools/cv-builder.njk`, `partials/cv-builder.njk`, `js/cv-builder.js`) lays a CV out on A4 pages in the browser and prints it as a PDF; its colour themes, CV headings in English, German and Albanian, and the example CVs live in `src/_data/cvBuilder.json`. A company website typed there is the one thing a tool sends out: its logo is loaded from Google's icon service (see Datenschutz). ATS CV (`tools/ats-cv.njk`, `partials/ats-cv.njk`, `js/ats-cv.js`, `src/_data/atsCv.json`) makes a plain one-column CV, matches it to a job ad and saves it as PDF, Word or text; it can start from an old CV, which `js/cv-import.js` reads in the browser from a PDF (with pdf.js, kept in `js/vendor/pdfjs/`), a Word file or pasted text. The job ad match knows words that mean the same skill in English, German and Albanian (`src/_data/atsSkills.json`, groups such as forklift licence, Staplerschein, pirun): a key word of the ad counts when the CV has any word of its group, and the chip says which (`AtsCv.findTerm`, marked ≈); a short line that reads like "Ihr Profil" or "Requirements" opens the part of the ad that lists what is required (`AtsCv.adParts`, closed by "Nice to have", "Wir bieten" and the like), and a key word from it weighs double in the score (`AtsCv.match`). It saves and opens JSON Resume files too (`AtsCv.toJsonResume`, `AtsCv.fromJsonResume`; jsonresume.org, schema 1.0.0): basics, work, education (certificates join it), skills and languages as the standard has them, dates as `2022-03` there and `03.2022` here, and what the standard has no place for (key achievements, extra sections, headings, the document's language) under `meta.stivenCatalyst`, so a file saved here opens here again whole; another tool's volunteer work, projects and awards become sections. "Download PDF" writes a PDF file of its own (`AtsCv.pdf`, with `js/pdf-kit.js`): the pages of the preview in points, Helvetica for Arial and Calibri and Times for Georgia, so the file is a few kilobytes and lays out the same in every reader and on every printer; the browser's print window stays under More as "Print the preview". Cover Letter (`tools/cover-letter.njk`, `partials/cover-letter.njk`, `js/cover-letter.js`, `src/_data/coverLetter.json`) writes the letter in the design of either CV and takes the details from them. Both write Word files with `js/docx-kit.js`. `js/pdf-kit.js` is the PDF writer: the fourteen standard fonts in WinAnsi (English, German and Albanian letters, the euro sign, dashes and quotes; a letter outside it loses its accent) with their widths, text, lines, and `PdfKit.flow`, which lays blocks (text with bold runs and bullets, a title with a date at the right, a rule, a table whose cells wrap in their columns, whose rows are never split between pages and whose header comes again on every page it runs on to) out in a column over as many pages as it needs, keeps a heading with what follows, splits a long paragraph between two lines and writes a footer on every page; nothing is embedded and no library is used. Tested in `tests/pdf-kit.test.cjs`. Shift Handover's "Save as PDF" (`ShiftHandoverPdf.sheetBlocks`, `sheetPdf`; `tests/handover-pdf.test.cjs`) writes the sheet with it: the numbers and the open issues as tables, the solved issues, heads-up and safety as bullets, the check and the takeover at the foot, in the page's language; the browser's print window stays as "Print". Pareto 80/20 (`tools/pareto.njk`, `partials/pareto.njk`, `js/pareto.js`, `src/_data/pareto.json`) ranks causes from a CSV, an Excel file (.xlsx, unzipped in the browser by `js/cv-import.js`), pasted cells or one of eleven templates for logistics, warehouse and hotel work; it lists every row it leaves out, merges spellings only when asked, and exports PNG, SVG and CSV. Damage Control, Incomplete Control and Delay Analyzer open their logs in it through `sessionStorage` (`sc-pareto-handoff`); its engine is tested in `tests/pareto.test.cjs`. Last-Mile CX Control Tower (`tools/cx-control-tower.njk`, `partials/cx-tower.njk`, `js/cx-tower.js`, `src/_data/cxTower.json`) reads four tables (deliveries, routes, incidents, actions) from CSV files or from the sheets of one Excel workbook, recognising each by its column names, and takes targets from a “Metric | Target” sheet. It computes Perfect Delivery per delivery, the other KPIs with their own denominators, partner and driver scorecards with a minimum sample, a route risk queue, a root-cause Pareto (drawn with the Pareto tool's chart and handed to it through `sessionStorage`), corrective actions and a weekly review; KPIs without an approved target are shown but never scored. Its Deep dive tab (`renderDeep` in `js/cx-tower.js`, the engine's `DEEP` table and `deepAggregate`, `deepSegments`, `deepWeekly`, `priorWindow`, `deepIncidents`) investigates one failure at a time (late, incomplete, damaged, temperature, first attempt, late without a message, complaints, not perfect; routes loading over target, leaving late, missing bags, gate open, cold scan missed, a safety deviation): rows without a valid outcome are left out, the failure rate is the numerator over its own eligible denominator and is bounded by the approved target of its KPI, the equal window before the period is compared, a breakdown by partner, shift, zone, week, driver, route (and ramp gate or vehicle for routes) ranks the segments by cases with their own rate and share and a “vs rest” difference only above the sample floor, a weekly column chart, the incidents whose type records that failure with how many name an affected row exactly, Gemba prompts per failure, the affected rows (CSV) and a management brief (copied or as Markdown). A clicked segment narrows the evidence only. The investigation workspace (title, stage, problem, evidence, where, five whys, process failure as a hypothesis until verified, containment, corrective and preventive action, owner, deadline, verification) is saved as a case (`cases` in the same `sc-cx-tower` state, so “All your work in one file” carries it; `caseCheck` refuses “verified effective” without a verified cause, evidence and a result), becomes an action in the tracker once (`caseToAction`; the action shows its investigation, the weekly review lists the open investigations), and the case register is exported and imported as CSV. The overview tiles and the root causes open the deep dive on their KPI. Its engine and the page (in jsdom) are tested in `tests/cx-tower.test.cjs`. The case thread (October 2026, phase 3 pilot) follows one investigation from the problem to the KPI verified: the investigation's id (`INV-…`) is the thread. "Create the linked action" puts the action in the station's register as before and, on the built page, in the Action Tracker too (`caseToTracker` → `ActionsStore.add`, with the case id as `caseId`, the owner, the deadline and the verification metric as the check; the tracker's id comes back as `trackerId` on the case; a second click adds nothing, `ActionsStore.forCase` lists a case's actions). "Work the whys in 5 Whys" opens the worksheet with the problem and `?case=`; the worksheet names the investigation and "Send the cause back" returns with `?case=&why1=…&root=`, which the tower reads once (`applyWhys`: empty fields are filled, written ones kept, the address cleaned). The tracker shows the investigation with a link back (`?case=INV-…` opens it), and an action is never verified without a result (`ActionsStore.update` refuses it; the tracker opens the form on the result and applies "verified" when one is saved). When the tracker's action is done, the case's stage reads "Verification pending" by itself, and the verification box measures the same failure before and after the change (`caseRows`: the snapshot's partner, shift, zone and segment; `afterWindow`: from the day the change took effect, which the visitor sets, to the last day with data; each rate over its own eligible rows). `verdict` says "effective" only with enough rows after the change (the deep dive's sample floor) and a lower rate (within the approved maximum, when there is one), "not effective" with enough rows and no fall, otherwise no verdict. "Close: verified effective" needs that verdict, the evidence, a verified cause and the action done; it writes the numbers into the case (`verification`), verifies the tracker's action with them as its result and marks the register row effective. "Reopen: not effective" keeps the action done with the result and lets the case take a new action under the same id. "Put the action on the shift handover" hands it over as an open issue whose text starts with the case id. Tested in `tests/case-thread.test.cjs` and, across the real pages, in `tests/browser/case-flow.spec.mjs`. Before the print window, all three CV tools show a short guide to saving the PDF (`js/print-guide.js`). The homepage points to the three career tools (`partials/career-feature.njk`). They run entirely in the browser; logs are kept only in the visitor's `localStorage`.
- The operations system (October 2026): sixteen tools that work as one daily machine, on three shared scripts. `js/chart-kit.js` (`window.ChartKit`) draws every new chart as one SVG in the page's colours: radar, columns, stacked columns (a Yamazumi), lines with control or specification limits, a histogram with its normal curve, box plots, a scatter with its fit, a heat map, a month calendar and a 2 × 2 matrix; `figure()` gives each one tooltip (pointer or tap) and the SVG and PNG buttons, exported in print colours; the trend charts of `tool-kit.js` share the same tooltip (`ToolKit.chartTooltip`). `js/workplace.js` (`window.Workplace`, `sc-workplace`, the same in every language) keeps the site, its shifts, its areas with their owners, its teams and the targets once, for every tool that offers them. `js/actions-store.js` (`window.ActionsStore`, `sc-actions`, the same in every language) is the one list of actions: a tool adds one directly or hands it over through the Action Tracker's address (`?action=&owner=&due=&source=&check=&area=&priority=`, read once and cleaned, never added twice), and the tracker shows them with their status (open, in progress, done, verified), the weekly review, a calendar file (.ics) and Excel. `js/stats-kit.js` (`window.StatsKit`) holds the statistics of Process Lab, tested against known values. The tools: Action Tracker, Daily Board (the SQDCP huddle board, filled from the other logs), 5S Audit (5S, 6S or 8S, with the red tag register), Safety Cross, A3 Report, Kaizen Board, OEE, Fishbone, FMEA (with the AIAG-VDA Action Priority), Process Lab (I-MR with the Nelson rules, capability, before/after tests, sample size), Time Study (takt and the Yamazumi), and Skills Matrix, Value Stream Map, Standard Work, Hotel KPIs and Gemba Walk. Each follows the same pattern as the tools before them: a page in the three languages, a partial, a data file per language, a script with its arithmetic on `window.<Name>Math` for the tests, a CSS part of its own (`src/css/13-…` to `28-…`), a test in `tests/<tool>.test.cjs`, a card and a planet. `node scripts/check-tool.mjs <tool>` renders a tool's three pages without the whole build and lists the texts that still lack a translation.
- Later additions to the operations system: Operations Report, Spaghetti Diagram, Project Portfolio, Lost & Found, Room Inspection and Maintenance Planner (assets, calendar or operating-hour intervals, PM compliance, MTBF/MTTR; `js/maintenance-planner.js`, `window.MaintenancePlannerMath`), and the 5S audit's before/after photos and audit plan with area rotation (`.ics`). `js/storage-guard.js` asks the browser to keep the tools' storage, shows how much space the work takes, and keeps restore points in IndexedDB (`sc-restore`): one a day, one before a file replaces anything, one every 15 minutes while the work changes ("recent", the last six kept, armed by typing so an idle page runs no timer) and one before a team sync. `js/team-sync.js` (`window.TeamSync`, `partials/team-sync.njk`, shown only when `site.sync.endpoint` is set) shares the tools a team ticks across its devices: a six-word team code gives the space id, the write token and an AES-GCM key (PBKDF2), the work is encrypted on the device, merged row by row (a row only one device changed keeps that change), and the server in `workers/sync/` (a Cloudflare Worker with one SQLite Durable Object per team; `node workers/sync/deploy.mjs`) stores only ciphertext. Tested in `tests/team-sync.test.cjs` and `tests/storage-guard.test.cjs`.
- The fourth round of the operations system (October 2026): Signals (`js/signals.js`: every tool's data read each morning; p, c, u and I-MR charts from `StatsKit` with the run rules, targets missed and overdue items, ranked), the hotel pack (Room Status with the standard housekeeping codes, Guest Complaints with LEARN recovery and complaints per 100 occupied room nights, the Housekeeping Planner with attendants needed and sections balanced by minutes), Leader Standard Work, Shift Capacity, ABC/XYZ Inventory (safety stock and reorder point from an inverse normal), SMED Changeover, the 8D Report, Risk Assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung, STOP order), Hoshin Kanri (X-matrix and bowling chart), Site Compare and the Print Pack. Each follows the tool pattern above, with its arithmetic on `window.<Name>Math`, a CSS part (`src/css/41-…` to `54-…`) and a test. Shift Pulse, the Daily Board and the Operations Report also read Room Inspection, the Maintenance Planner and Lost & Found, and OEE breakdowns reach the planner. Team sync gained read-only codes (`scr1.` + the space id and key, without the write token, so the server refuses their writes), a new team code (`TeamSync.rotate`) and `TeamSync.readTeamCopy` for Site Compare; its QR encoder reaches version 40 (level M, 2331 bytes), with the codeword table and the alignment pattern centres computed from the version rather than copied, so a handover link fits too; reminders of due actions stay on the device (`js/reminders.js`, IndexedDB `sc-reminders`, a periodic sync in `sw.js` where supported). `ImportMap.PRESETS` recognises typical column names of PMS, warehouse and route exports; floor mode (`js/floor-mode.js`, `sc-floor-mode-ui`, `src/css/52-floor-mode.css`) enlarges the tool pages for gloves and one hand; charts print dark on paper whatever the theme. In `eleventy.config.js`, data files read from JSON are frozen at their top level, so Eleventy shares them between pages instead of copying them into each: the build needs a third of the memory it did.
- The planets are roles. The homepage hero (`partials/cosmos-hero.njk`, `js/cosmos.js`) draws the Catalyst light with one planet per role on its orbits. The bulb hangs from the top of the hero and swings like a pendant lamp when the system is dragged (a pendulum with gravity and damping, and a light tilt with the mouse), throwing a pool of light on the plane of the orbits that moves with it; its filament is a sound wave, the voice of the brand, which pulses as it glows and speaks up when the bulb is tapped. A small reader, a pictogram without a face (one warm colour, thick rounded limbs, the head apart from the body), sits on two books on the inner orbit in the pool of light and reads an open book, turning a page now and then; it looks up, stretches or walks a few steps, and left alone for a minute it dozes off over its book while the light dims a little (the pointer wakes it). When a planet is opened it runs there along the beam and stands on it, leaving the books at its seat, when a moon is tapped it jumps to the moon as the tool opens, and when the planet is closed it comes back and sits down; with reduced motion it only sits, or stands on the open planet. The planets sit, and an orbit widens as the role's view does: Pulse is the Shift Lead (one shift), Zenith the Area Manager (one area, week by week), Lumen the Process Manager (the whole system), and Atlas the career that every role carries. The roles live in `src/_data/families.json`: name, role, scope, colour and orbit; `tools` (the tools whose home is this planet), `learn` (training modules, such as the Six Sigma course on Lumen), `also` (tools borrowed from other planets), `read` (essays, by file slug), `routine` (when each tool is used: a moment, what to do, which tool) and `next` (the next orbit). Each planet has its own physics and its moons are its tools (one moon per tool or training module): Pulse cracks and beats inside a ring that is a heartbeat trace, Zenith is a contour map under a radar sweep with a polar cap, Lumen shines by itself inside a ring of dust in five parts (the DMAIC steps), Atlas is a globe with meridians, half in the light, with the path of a career drawn as a constellation on its lit side. The same four planets are drawn as SVG on the role cards, the Tools page and the role pages (`partials/family-planet.njk`, shapes from `src/_data/familySky.js`: `planets`), where Pulse beats, Zenith's radar sweeps and Lumen's ring turns in CSS. Dragging turns and tilts the system, a tap on a planet or its button opens that role's tools and a link to its planet, and a tap on a moon opens that tool (the mouse shows its name first; the moons are read from the panel's list, so they stay the same links). It pauses when off screen and stays still for visitors who prefer reduced motion. Every role has its own page, `/roles/<slug>/` in each language (`src/roles.njk`, `src/de/roles.njk`, `src/sq/roles.njk`, all through `partials/role-page.njk`), in four rings: Use (its tools and the ones it borrows), Routine, Learn (training modules and essays) and Grow (the next orbit, and Atlas for the CV). The Tools page shows the same roles (`partials/tool-families.njk`): a card per role with its own planet (`partials/family-cards.njk`, also on the homepage, where each card opens the role's page), then each role with its tools. A tool that no role lists yet appears under "More tools"; `tests/families.test.cjs` checks that every tool has exactly one home in all three languages, that borrowed tools and routine steps exist, and that every text is translated. Every tool page carries its role (`familyOf` in `eleventy.config.js`, set in `layouts/base.njk`): a drawn background above the title (`partials/family-stage.njk`, shapes from `src/_data/familySky.js`, fitted to the title by `js/family-sky.js`), a badge that opens the role's page, and "More from …" with the role's other tools at the end (`partials/family-more.njk`). Lumen has a beam of light with drifting dust, Pulse a monitor grid and a heartbeat along the foot of the title, Zenith contour lines and a radar, Atlas a constellation and a compass; a training module has five moons that light up as the DMAIC steps are completed. The role's own planet (the same SVG as on the cards) stands in the open space beside the title on every tool and role page, and on a training module at the start of the moons' arc; `js/family-sky.js` measures the title's lines, the eyebrow, the badge and the fixed parts of the drawing and moves the planet when its body (disc, rings and moons, not its glow) would touch a word: it stays where the stylesheet puts it when it can, else goes beside the title past its longest line, or above it towards the right, at full size first and then smaller, and on a phone the corner above the title comes first. The decision is written to `data-spot` on the planet, and `#sky-debug` in the address adds a `data-trace`. The drawings stay around the title, stop for reduced motion and are left out of print. Inside the tool the role colour is the accent too (labels, links, focus, selected answers, progress and the main buttons), while colours that carry meaning (right and wrong, better and worse, priority, warnings, the ticks in Pulse) keep a separate signal colour and the CV documents keep their own themes.
